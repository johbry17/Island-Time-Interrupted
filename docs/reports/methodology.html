Methodology

Research design, analytical decisions, and limitations

Research Design

This study examines two questions:

  1. Was greater pre-pandemic tourism exposure associated with more severe economic contractions in 2020?
  2. Was tourism exposure associated with economic recovery by 2024?

The analysis uses exploratory cross-sectional OLS regression and correlation to examine the 2020 shock, followed by indexed GDP comparisons to assess recovery.

The models are bivariate and descriptive. They do not control for economic structure, fiscal capacity, pandemic restrictions, or policy responses. Results identify associations, not causal effects.

Sample Construction

The candidate universe contains 58 economies based on the November 2026 UN-OHRLLS SIDS classification, including 18 Associate Member SIDS and BES as an additional island economy. The analytical panel contains 57 economies; BES has no retrieved observations.

Sample Inclusion rule N
Candidate universe All candidates 58
Analytical panel At least one source observation 57
Tourism-data sample Tourism observation in 2017–2019 48
OWID shock sample OWID baseline and 2020 GDP growth 29
WDI shock sample WDI baseline and 2020 GDP growth 35
Common shock sample Both indicators and 2020 GDP growth 24
Recovery sample OWID shock sample with 2019 and subsequent GDP 29

Sample membership is determined using the 2017–2019 tourism window and 2020 GDP growth. Membership remains fixed when calculating the alternative 2015–2019 baseline. Economy-level coverage and exclusions are recorded in country_coverage.csv.

Tourism Baselines

Two indicators are used: OWID visitor spending (% GDP) and WDI tourism receipts (% exports).

Specification Years
Primary baseline 2015–2019
Sensitivity baseline 2017–2019

For each economy, the baseline is the arithmetic mean of available annual observations:

\[ \bar{x}_i = \frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{t \in T_i}x_{it} \]

Missing years are not imputed. Contributing observation counts are retained, and one-observation baselines are flagged as thin.

The primary 2015–2019 specification was selected after examining the 2017–2019 results, rather than preregistered. Both specifications are reported.

Shock Models

The outcome is 2020 real GDP growth (WDI NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG). Tourism exposure is entered separately using each indicator and baseline.

\[ GDPGrowth_{i,2020} = \beta_0+\beta_1 TourismExposure_i+\epsilon_i \]

Eight principal specifications vary by tourism indicator, sample (indicator-specific or common), and baseline window.

Models report sample size, slope, 95% confidence interval, R-squared, Pearson and Spearman correlations, and raw and Holm-adjusted p-values. Holm adjustment is applied across the eight principal model tests.

Sensitivity Analysis

Cook’s distance, using the conventional (4/N) reference threshold, identifies potentially influential observations. The most influential economy is excluded in separate diagnostic reruns; principal estimates retain all eligible economies.

Additional models require at least three annual observations per tourism baseline. This reduces the WDI 2017–2019 full sample from 35 to 21 economies. Restricted estimates are reported separately.

Recovery Measures

Real GDP and real GDP per capita are indexed independently to each economy’s 2019 value:

\[ Index_{i,t} = \frac{Value_{i,t}}{Value_{i,2019}}\times100 \]

2019 is the last full pre-pandemic year. The principal comparison uses 2024, when 28 of 29 recovery-sample economies have observations. The U.S. Virgin Islands is missing.

2025 observations cover 22 economies and are preliminary; they are excluded from the principal comparison.

Aggregate GDP and GDP per capita are reported separately. They measure total output and output relative to population, respectively, and do not establish changes in household welfare or the distribution of economic recovery.

Limitations and Reproducibility

No missing observations are imputed. The analysis is constrained by uneven source coverage, differing tourism definitions, variable baseline completeness, and the limitations of annual national GDP measures.

The bivariate models cannot isolate tourism exposure from other economic and policy factors. The recovery indices measure output relative to 2019, not employment, inequality, or household well-being.

Analysis was conducted in R and rendered with Quarto. Principal packages include dplyr, tidyr, wbstats, ggplot2, scales, here, and knitr.

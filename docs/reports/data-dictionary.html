Data Dictionary

Sources, Indicators, Coverage, and Limitations

This page documents the data behind Island Time, Interrupted: where the information came from, what each indicator measures, how the datasets are structured, and what limitations affect their use.

The Methodology explains how these data were selected, combined, and analyzed. The Technical Analysis presents the findings.

Tourism Indicators

International visitor spending (% of GDP)

Primary indicator

Field Description
Source UN Tourism statistics, national statistical organizations and central banks, OECD national accounts, and World Bank staff estimates, processed by Our World in Data
Publisher Our World in Data
Analytical variable visitor_spend_gdp_pct
Baseline variables vspend_mean_1519, vspend_mean_1719
Unit Percent of GDP
Available years 1995–2024
Use Primary explanatory indicator for shock analysis

What it measures

Spending by international visitors in the destination economy, expressed relative to the economy’s GDP. It includes spending on accommodation, food, transport, entertainment, shopping, and fares paid to domestic airlines.

The indicator provides a measure of the scale of international visitor spending relative to the overall economy. A higher value suggests greater exposure to international visitor demand.

Use in this project

This is the primary explanatory indicator in the shock analysis. Its relatively broad coverage makes it more suitable for cross-economy comparison than the available tourism direct GDP series.

International tourism receipts (% of total exports)

Field Description
Source World Bank, World Development Indicators (WDI)
Indicator code ST.INT.RCPT.XP.ZS
Analytical variable tourism_receipts_pct_exports
Baseline variables receipts_mean_1519, receipts_mean_1719
Unit Percent of total exports of goods and services
Available years Approximately 1995–2020
Use Complementary explanatory indicator for shock analysis

What it measures

International tourism receipts relative to the total value of exports of goods and services. Receipts represent payments made to a destination economy by international visitors for goods and services consumed during their trips.

Coverage limitations

Fourteen of the 35 economies in the WDI shock sample lack a 2019 observation. Their 2017–2019 baselines therefore rely on 2017 and 2018 only.

This uneven coverage is important when comparing the WDI results with those from OWID.

Use in this project

A complementary tourism exposure measure. It is used in its own shock sample and in the common-sample comparison, allowing the analysis to test whether the observed relationship depends on the choice of tourism indicator.

Economic Indicators

Economic outcome data come primarily from the World Bank’s World Development Indicators.

GDP growth (annual %)

Field Description
Source World Bank, WDI
Indicator code NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG
Analytical variable gdp_growth_pct
Derived variable gdp_growth_pct_2020
Unit Annual percent change
Prices Constant 2015 prices
Use Outcome variable for 2020 shock regressions

What it measures

The annual percentage change in real gross domestic product. It captures the change in the volume of economic output, excluding the effects of price changes.

Use in this project

The primary outcome variable in the 2020 shock regressions. It measures the economy-wide contraction during the first pandemic year.

Limitations

Annual GDP growth aggregates activity across the entire calendar year. It does not capture the precise timing of travel restrictions, differences in the duration of economic disruption, or the distribution of economic losses among households and industries.

It also cannot distinguish pandemic-related effects from other economic developments occurring in the same year.

Real GDP (constant 2015 USD)

Field Description
Source World Bank, WDI
Indicator code NY.GDP.MKTP.KD
Analytical variable gdp_real_const2015usd
Unit Constant 2015 US dollars
Use Outcome variable for recovery analysis

What it measures

The total value of domestic production, expressed in constant 2015 US dollars. Constant-price values remove the effect of changes in prices over time, allowing comparisons of real output across years.

Use in this project

The primary measure of aggregate recovery. Each economy’s annual value is indexed to its 2019 level, with 2019 set equal to 100.

Coverage

Within the 29-economy shock sample:

  • 2015–2022: 29 economies
  • 2023–2024: 28 economies
  • 2025: 22 economies, with preliminary estimates

Real GDP per capita (constant 2015 USD)

Field Description
Source World Bank, WDI
Indicator code NY.GDP.PCAP.KD
Analytical variable gdppc_real_const2015usd
Unit Constant 2015 US dollars per person
Use Per-capita economic recovery

What it measures

Real GDP divided by the population, expressed in constant 2015 US dollars per person.

Use in this project

A complementary recovery measure. It distinguishes the recovery of total economic output from the recovery of output relative to population.

Candidate Universe and Classification

The candidate universe is based on the UN-OHRLLS list of Small Island Developing States.

Classification Count Description
OHRLLS-listed SIDS 39 Economies formally included on the UN SIDS list
Associate Member SIDS 18 Associate members of UN Regional Commissions included in the SIDS group
Other island economy 1 Bonaire, Sint Eustatius, and Saba (BES), included for regional completeness
Candidate universe 58

Several classification details are worth preserving:

  • Cook Islands and Niue: Included on the OHRLLS SIDS list despite not being UN General Assembly members. They are sovereign states in free association with New Zealand.
  • Bahrain: No longer classified as a United Nations Small Island Developing State.
  • Associate members: Mostly non-sovereign territories and dependencies associated with UN Regional Commissions.
  • BES: Dutch special municipalities, not included on the OHRLLS list. Retained as an additional island economy but absent from the analytical panel because no retrieved source provided observations.

The candidate universe should not be confused with the regression samples. Data availability determines which economies can contribute to each analysis.

Processed Datasets

The raw sources are transformed into a set of processed datasets that support sample construction, analysis, and visualization. Repo location: data/processed/.

candidates.csv

Unit: One row per candidate economy (58 rows).

Contains the candidate universe and its classification.

Variable Description
iso3 Three-letter economy code
country Economy name
sids_region Caribbean, Pacific, or AIMS
sids_status Classification within the SIDS universe
political_status Political or territorial status
source Classification source
notes Additional classification details

country_coverage.csv

Unit: One row per candidate economy (58 rows).

Documents the availability of source observations and the resulting sample membership.

Includes observation counts for the relevant tourism indicators and binary flags identifying membership in the OWID shock sample, WDI shock sample, common sample, and recovery sample.

This file is the principal record of why an economy is included in or excluded from a particular analysis.

baseline_shock_recovery.csv

This is the principal economy-level dataset used to assemble the shock and recovery analyses.

Unit: One row per economy in the analytical panel (57 rows).

Combines the constructed tourism baselines, observation counts, economic outcomes, and sample-membership flags.

Includes:

  • 2015–2019 and 2017–2019 tourism-indicator means
  • Annual observation counts contributing to each baseline
  • Economic outcome values for 2015–2025
  • Flags identifying the relevant analytical samples

analytical_panel.csv

Unit: One economy-year observation.

A long-format, unbalanced panel containing 1,707 rows across 57 economies and years 1995–2025.

The dataset combines the available OWID and WDI indicators into a common structure. Coverage varies by indicator, economy, and year. Consequently, many rows contain missing values for individual variables.

This is not a balanced panel: not every economy has an observation for every year.

BES is absent because no source returned observations for it.

recovery_index.csv

Unit: One economy-year observation.

Contains indexed real GDP and real GDP per capita for the 29-economy primary shock sample.

Variable Description
gdp_index Aggregate real GDP, 2019 = 100
gdppc_index Real GDP per capita, 2019 = 100

The dataset covers 2015–2025, with annual coverage declining in later years. The 2025 observations cover 22 economies and include preliminary source estimates.

Coverage and Comparability

The two principal tourism indicators have different coverage profiles. That difference affects which economies can be compared and how much confidence to place in comparisons across specifications.

Indicator Latest year in current retrieval Key coverage issue
OWID visitor spending (% GDP) 2024 Broadest coverage in the shock sample
WDI tourism receipts (% exports) 2020 No observations after 2020; 14 of 35 WDI shock-sample economies lack 2019
WDI real GDP 2025 2025 coverage limited to 22 economies and preliminary

The common sample of 24 economies allows the two principal tourism indicators to be compared without changing the country set.

Even within that sample, the indicators can produce different estimates because their denominators and underlying coverage differ.

Missing data should not be interpreted as zero tourism, zero economic activity, or absence of an economic relationship. It means the relevant source did not provide an observation suitable for that comparison.

Source Notes and Limitations

Several limitations apply across the data inventory:

  • Different statistical concepts: Gross visitor spending and tourism receipts measure different things. Their values should not be substituted for one another.
  • Uneven national reporting: International tourism statistics depend on national collection and reporting systems. Definitions and coverage can vary.
  • Incomplete territorial coverage: Some non-sovereign territories and smaller island economies are not recognized by the World Bank API or are missing from particular OWID datasets.
  • Uneven time coverage: Tourism and economic indicators do not necessarily cover the same years or economies. The analysis documents these differences rather than filling gaps with assumed values.
  • Revisions and preliminary estimates: Source datasets can be revised. The 2025 GDP observations are preliminary in the current retrieval and should not be treated as final.
  • National aggregates: GDP and tourism indicators describe economy-wide activity. They do not directly measure the distribution of gains and losses among residents.

The processed datasets preserve the distinctions between source observations, constructed baselines, and indexed outcomes. The Methodology documents the transformations and inclusion decisions applied to them.
